# Course repository collaboration standard

This public repository is both a learning environment and a maintained
software project. Every participant should be able to propose, question,
review, revise, and occasionally make mistakes without being demeaned or
exposed.

## Expected conduct

- Critique code, evidence, interfaces, and claims rather than people.
- Make review comments specific, actionable, and proportional to risk.
- Ask when context is missing; do not present preference as correctness.
- Give credit for ideas, code, data, reviews, and debugging assistance.
- Respect different levels of prior experience and explain unfamiliar tools.
- Protect private student information, credentials, restricted data, and
  communications intended to remain private.
- Use accessible language and formats, and respond constructively when an
  accessibility barrier is identified.
- Disclose relevant uncertainty, limitations, conflicts of interest, and use
  of generated or externally sourced material.

Harassment, discriminatory language, threats, deliberate humiliation,
impersonation, plagiarism, exposing private information, sabotaging another
person's work, and knowingly bypassing repository security controls are not
acceptable.

## Review disagreements

Technical disagreement is normal. State the contract or risk at issue, provide
evidence when possible, and separate blocking correctness or safety concerns
from optional suggestions. If a discussion stops producing understanding,
pause it and ask the instructor or designated maintainer to mediate.

Do not use public issues or pull requests to discuss grades, accommodations,
conduct reports, personal circumstances, or confidential course matters.

## Reporting and response

Report ordinary technical concerns in the relevant issue or pull request.
Report security problems and exposed private information through the private
route in `SECURITY.md`. Report conduct or course concerns privately to the
instructor through the institutional course channel. When the instructor is
involved in the concern, use the appropriate Rice University course or
institutional reporting channel.

Repository access, review participation, or contribution acceptance may be
limited when necessary to protect people, data, course integrity, or system
security. Course and university policies continue to apply; this document
defines repository collaboration expectations rather than replacing those
policies.
