\section{A metric encodes a scientific judgment}

Euclidean distance
$d(x,x')=\sqrt{\sum_j(x_j-x'_j)^2}$ compares coordinate differences. A change
of units changes that comparison. Standardizing features makes one empirical
standard deviation comparable across coordinates, but it does not prove that
those deviations carry equal scientific meaning.

Categorical, circular, spatial, graph, and functional data may require other
representations. A metric should respect relevant invariances: translating a
molecule, permuting exchangeable objects, or shifting a periodic phase should
not arbitrarily change similarity when the task says it should not.

\section{Nearest neighbors defer fitting}

For regression, $k$-nearest neighbors averages targets among the $k$ closest
training points. For classification, it votes or averages class indicators.
Small $k$ adapts locally but has high variance. Large $k$ smooths more strongly
and can erase local structure.

Training may consist mainly of storing data, but inference must search those
data. Index structures can accelerate low-dimensional queries; high-dimensional
geometry often makes exact search expensive and neighborhoods uninformative.
Approximate search trades accuracy for latency and requires its own evaluation.

Irrelevant dimensions are particularly harmful because distances concentrate
and true neighbors become hard to distinguish. This is one manifestation of the
curse of dimensionality: local coverage requires rapidly increasing sample size
as dimension grows.

\section{Margins produce a different geometry}

For labels $y_i\in\{-1,1\}$, a linear separator predicts from
$w^Tx+b$. The geometric margin of a point is
$y_i(w^Tx_i+b)/\lVert w\rVert$. A hard-margin support-vector machine chooses the
separating hyperplane with maximum minimum margin when separation is possible.

The soft-margin formulation permits violations through hinge loss:
\[
\min_{w,b}\quad \frac{1}{2}\lVert w\rVert^2
+C\sum_{i=1}^n\max(0,1-y_i(w^Tx_i+b)).
\]
The parameter $C$ controls the penalty for violations relative to margin size.
Only points on or inside the margin influence the final boundary directly;
these are the support vectors.

Feature scaling changes both margins and regularization. A support-vector
pipeline therefore owns its fitted preprocessing. A margin score is not a
calibrated probability without an additional validated calibration procedure.

\section{Kernels change the feature space implicitly}

A kernel $K(x,x')=\langle\phi(x),\phi(x')\rangle$ computes an inner product in
a transformed feature space without necessarily constructing every coordinate
of $\phi$. Polynomial and radial-basis kernels create nonlinear boundaries in
the original space.

The kernel trick does not remove model selection. Kernel family, scale, and
regularization determine smoothness and complexity. Kernel matrices grow
quadratically with sample count, making classical kernel methods unsuitable for
some large datasets. Approximation or a different model class may be preferable.

\begin{misconceptionbox}
\textbf{Misconception:} an RBF kernel automatically discovers the right
nonlinearity. \textbf{Correction:} it imposes a particular similarity with a
chosen length scale. Poor scaling, high dimension, extrapolation, or an
inappropriate invariance can make that geometry scientifically wrong.
\end{misconceptionbox}

\section{Evaluation includes resource behavior}

Compare geometric methods using the same outer split and preprocessing
boundary. Validation chooses $k$, distance weighting, $C$, and kernel parameters.
The report also measures artifact size, fit time, prediction latency across
batch sizes, and memory. A method with excellent average error but unbounded
tail latency may violate the product contract.

\begin{workedexample}{when battery similarity fails}
Suppose early-cycle features include voltage in volts, resistance in milliohms,
and cycle duration in seconds. Unscaled Euclidean kNN is dominated by the
largest numerical range. Standardization improves validation performance, but a
new manufacturing batch lies far outside the training resistance range.

The team reports this distance-to-training evidence rather than forcing a
confident neighbor vote. It compares a scaled kNN pipeline with a linear and an
RBF support-vector model on held-out batches. The selection report includes
error, calibration where probabilities are needed, inference latency, and the
fraction of queries outside a prespecified support region.
\end{workedexample}

\begin{chapterreview}
Geometric learners make similarity assumptions visible. kNN trades local bias
against variance and prediction cost. Support-vector machines trade margin
against violations, while kernels define an implicit feature geometry. Scaling,
invariance, dimensionality, support, memory, and latency are model properties,
not peripheral preprocessing details.
\end{chapterreview}
